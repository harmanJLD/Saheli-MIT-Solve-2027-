For women without a smartphone or data

Only about half of Indian women use a mobile phone themselves, and many share it. So Saheli also works on any basic phone: she gives a free missed call, Saheli calls back with a voice menu in her language, and alerts come by SMS. Press Missed call, then use the keypad.

Safeguards

Working in this prototype

  • Saheli sorts and refers. It never diagnoses or prescribes.
  • Every score shows its reasons.
  • Nothing goes to the ASHA without the woman's yes.
  • Crisis words always show Tele-MANAS 14416 and 112, even if she declines sharing.
  • Quick exit wipes the conversation and shows a neutral screen.
  • The health card QR holds only a code, not health details.

Planned for the pilot (not yet built)

  • Human counselor review queue for crisis and violence cases.
  • Volunteer gynecologist and counselor call-backs, through partners.
  • Health card accepted at clinics near sugar factories, through district agreements.
  • Accuracy checked separately for tribal, Dalit and other marginalized groups.
  • De-identified pooling of data for district patterns.
  • PIN or disguised entry for shared phones.

Next milestones

  • Retrain the anemia model on NFHS-5 survey microdata.
  • Label real conversations with ASHAs and gynecologists during the Beed pilot.
  • Validate the priority list against visit outcomes.
